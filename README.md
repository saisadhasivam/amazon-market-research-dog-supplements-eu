# Amazon Market Research & Competitive Analysis: European Dog Supplements/Longevity Brand

**Author:** Sai Sadhasivam
**Date:** August 2026
**Type:** Market research and competitive intelligence brief (client-commissioned, Upwork)

---

## Overview

This repository contains a structured market research and competitive analysis project supporting the exploratory launch of a premium dog health, longevity, and wellness supplement brand in Europe, with Germany and Amazon.de as the initial test market and channel, followed by expansion across major European markets.

The objective was to move beyond generic AI-generated market summaries and unsupported market-size figures, and instead build a fully traceable research base: every claim, figure, and product data point backed by a dated, publicly accessible source, structured so a client can audit any statement in the final report back to its origin.

The final deliverable set is a full written market research report, a standalone executive summary and launch strategy document, a 30-tab consolidated Excel database of underlying competitor and market data, and a complete source list, covering market sizing, Amazon.de competitive analysis, category demand and competition, a longevity/healthspan deep dive, brand and competitor analysis, customer review mining, product format and pricing analysis, German keyword and search demand, regulatory requirements, an 11-criterion opportunity scoring matrix, and a 5-product launch recommendation with a phased go-to-market strategy.

---

## Research Questions

The research was designed to answer six core questions, set before any data collection began:

1. **How big is the opportunity, and where?** What is the current size, growth rate, and structure of the dog supplement/nutraceutical market across Germany and the other target European markets?
2. **Who already has ground, and where are the gaps?** Who are the leading competitors on Amazon.de and in the wider German/European market, and what does their product, pricing, and positioning look like?
3. **What do customers actually want?** What are customers not getting from existing products, based on direct review mining rather than assumption?
4. **Where is demand strong but competition weak?** Which product categories show high demand but weak competition, and which formats and price bands are viable for a new premium entrant?
5. **Is longevity a real category, or too early?** Is longevity/healthy aging a credible, distinct opportunity in Europe today, specifically for the client's stated priority focus?
6. **What should actually be launched, in what order?** Given all of the above, which products should a new premium brand launch first, in what sequence, and why?

These questions shaped every research section below and are answered directly in the final report's Executive Summary and Sections 14-15.

## Analytical Framework

"High-opportunity category" is not a self-evident term, so before scoring began, it was operationalized into an 11-criterion Opportunity Matrix, fixed in writing in the project's Scope Note before any deep-dive research started:

| # | Criterion | Scoring Direction |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Market demand | Higher demand = higher score |
| 2 | Growth trajectory | Higher growth = higher score |
| 3 | Competition | **Inverted** - lower/less saturated competition = higher score |
| 4 | Average selling price | Higher achievable price = higher score |
| 5 | Margin potential | Higher margin = higher score |
| 6 | Differentiation potential | More room to differentiate = higher score |
| 7 | Repeat-purchase potential | Higher repeat/subscription fit = higher score |
| 8 | Regulatory complexity | **Inverted** - lower complexity = higher score |
| 9 | Amazon opportunity | Higher = higher score |
| 10 | DTC opportunity | Higher = higher score |
| 11 | Long-term brand potential | Higher = higher score |

Ten categories (of the sixteen named in the original brief) were scored 1-5 against all eleven criteria, for a maximum possible score of 55. Two categories, antioxidants/metabolic health and weight management, were explicitly excluded from scoring rather than silently dropped, because research found they do not function as standalone product categories in this market. The full scoring rationale, including why competition and regulatory complexity are scored in inverted form, is documented in the Opportunity Matrix's own "Scoring Key" tab, preserved in the repository rather than summarized away.

## Process

The research followed a six-stage discipline, each stage producing its own auditable artifact before the next stage began:

**1. Scope definition.** Geographic scope, category scope, the six research questions, and the 11-criterion analytical framework were fixed in writing (`Scope_Note.txt`) before any company or product research started.

**2. Source collection (11 sections).** Research was conducted across eleven fixed areas, mirroring how a competitive intelligence analyst would structure a market-entry study: European market sizing, a Germany deep dive, Amazon.de competitive product analysis, category-level demand and competition, a longevity/healthspan deep dive, competitor and brand analysis, customer review mining, product format analysis, pricing analysis, German keyword and search demand, and a regulatory and market entry overview. Every factual claim is logged individually with its source title, URL, date accessed, and whether it is verified or an estimate (`Data_Source_Documentation.xlsx`), so no statement in the final report rests on an untraceable assertion.

**3. Synthesis.** Sourced facts across all eleven sections were condensed into a single working matrix, one row per product category (`Master_Analysis.xlsx`), which fed directly into the opportunity scoring exercise and the narrative report. Keeping the matrix separate from the prose ensured the writing stayed anchored to sourced facts rather than drifting into unsupported generalization.

**4. Opportunity scoring.** Each category was scored against the fixed 11-criterion framework (`Opportunity_Matrix.xlsx`), ranked, and color-coded by opportunity tier, so the final product recommendations are a documented, criteria-based output rather than an unexplained pick.

**5. Product recommendations and launch sequencing.** Five launch products were specified in full (concept, target customer, ingredients, format, price, competitors, market gap, USP, risks) and sequenced into a phased Germany-first and European expansion strategy (`Product_Recommendations_and_Launch_Strategy.xlsx`). The launch order deliberately balances the matrix ranking against practical risk: the top-scoring categories are not always launched first, and the reasoning for every sequencing decision is documented, not asserted.

**6. Deliverable drafting and finalization.** The working analysis was converted into a full written report, a standalone executive summary, a complete source list, and a consolidated 30-tab Excel database, each cross-checked against the underlying working files for consistency before being finalized.

## Sourcing Standards

Only publicly available, credible sources were used: live Amazon.de and Amazon.com product listings, company and brand websites, professional market research firms, official government and regulatory sources (including Germany's BVL and EUR-Lex for EU law), and reputable news and trade publications. No paid Amazon research tool (Helium 10, Jungle Scout, or Keepa) was available for this research pass; where the original brief specifically requested tool-verified data such as exact Best Seller Rank or monthly unit sales, this limitation is stated directly in the report rather than presented as verified fact.

Verified data is distinguished from estimates throughout the source log and the final report. Where sources disagreed, for example on European market size (estimates ranged from roughly EUR0.78 billion to EUR1.96 billion depending on category definition), the discrepancy is stated rather than silently resolved. Where a research finding was later found to be incomplete, for example an initial "zero longevity competition" finding that was subsequently corrected once a direct competitor's own longevity product line was identified, the correction is documented in place rather than quietly overwritten. This is a market and business-positioning study, not a technical veterinary or formulation-science document, and it was scoped that way deliberately.

## Auditability

Every figure, claim, and product data point in the final report traces back through `Master_Analysis.xlsx` and `Opportunity_Matrix.xlsx` to an individually logged, sourced entry in `Data_Source_Documentation.xlsx`, and from there to its original public source, with a source title, URL, and date accessed. The consolidated `Competitor_and_Market_Database.xlsx` in `03_Deliverables` preserves this same underlying data, tab by tab, alongside the final report, so the client can verify any recommendation in the report without needing to reconstruct the research separately. Nothing in the final report rests on a claim that cannot be walked back to a named, dated, publicly accessible source, and known data gaps (for example, figures that would require a paid Amazon research tool to verify precisely) are stated directly in the report rather than presented as settled fact.
